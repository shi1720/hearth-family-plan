HEARTH | THREE-MINUTE DEMO
Verbatim narration and separate shot list
Prepared for Shivam Gupta

NARRATION | 3:00 target

Having a will does not mean the people you love can carry out your wishes. The original might be missing. A beneficiary instruction might be outdated. A trusted helper might not know what they are allowed to see.

I'm Shivam Gupta. I built Hearth with AI assistance to connect family preparation to the practical work that follows. Maria and every record in this demonstration are fictional.

Maria wants her children, Elena and James, to share fairly. But an old retirement statement names only Elena. Hearth keeps her wish separate from the institution's recorded instruction. It creates a question to resolve, rather than changing a beneficiary.

Here, I attach an example PDF and explicitly consent to Meta processing it. Muse Spark 1.3 reads the attachment. I check the transcription against the original file before accepting it. Muse Wonder then reviews the connected plan for gaps across documents, people, and account records.

These proposals include exact source quotes. I can inspect the evidence, reject a suggestion, or accept it as a practical next step. Acceptance creates work to review; it does not make the AI's interpretation legally binding.

Now I preview Elena's limited access. She can find permitted care information, while private account details and personal wishes stay hidden. An adviser funding the service does not automatically receive those details either.

Back in Maria's view, I record the institution's confirmation reference. The task now has completion evidence. If material account information changes later, the previous confirmation needs review again.

When the plan is needed, reporting a death and establishing authority are separate steps. A helper invitation is not a court appointment.

For a life-policy claim, Hearth records the claimant's separate authority, insurer requirements, and next action. I export a preparation packet. Submission and payment need their own evidence; an approved claim is not money received.

The estate ledger separately tracks confirmed funds, proposed entries, and a held reserve. It does not move money. Maria's giving intention also remains an intention until the appropriate instruction and receipt are recorded.

Our proposed buyer is an independent advice firm: eighteen thousand dollars annually for a bounded, two-hundred-and-fifty-household service. That price and the savings still need customer validation.

Hearth does not execute wills, file claims, or replace professionals. It helps families know what is ready, what is private, and what still needs doing.
