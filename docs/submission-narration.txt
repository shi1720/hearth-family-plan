After someone dies, the people who loved them are left with questions. Where is the will? Who can act? Which claim comes next? Grief becomes a paperwork project.

Hearth helps families prepare before that moment, and gives trusted helpers and advice firms a clear path through what follows. One private workspace, from preparation to handover to follow-through.

Meet Maria and Elena, a fictional mother and daughter. Maria has a will, accounts, care notes and wishes. Elena needs to know what matters, where it is, and what she is allowed to see.

First, prepare. Record trusted people, locate original documents, and compare account instructions with current wishes. Maria wants both children included, but an old statement names only Elena. Hearth keeps that mismatch visible.

With permission, Meta's Muse Spark one point three reads Maria's sample policy. The transcription stays unconfirmed until a person checks it against the file.

Muse Wonder one point zero then connects the records. It notices that the policy names Elena while the account summary says the beneficiary is unknown, and proposes an insurer confirmation.

Each proposal keeps an exact source quote. A person can reject it or create a task, with a responsible person and completion evidence. The model suggests. People decide.

Next, handover. Elena's emergency view shows permitted care information, while private accounts and personal letters stay hidden. An invitation gives access to information, not legal authority.

After a death, record the report, then separately review evidence of death, identity and appointment. The proper role and evidence are required for restricted administration. The family can see which step remains unresolved.

Now follow through. For an insurance claim, organise the claimant's authority, required documents, next action and preparation packet. Track submission, insurer approval and actual receipt separately, so an unfinished claim cannot look complete.

The estate ledger separates proposed expenses, confirmed transactions and reserves. Giving stays optional, with separate intention, execution and receipt stages. Hearth does not move money or replace professional judgment.

Meta turns scattered records into reviewable work. Deterministic rules protect permissions and evidence states. The evaluation page runs those rules and checks live model output against its source, without claiming legal accuracy.

The buyer is an advice firm coordinating families over time. Our proposed annual service is eighteen thousand dollars for two hundred and fifty households. At sixty dollars per staff hour, three hundred hours saved would cover the annual fee. Those savings need customer validation.

Hearth connects preparation, careful access, claims and follow-through, so families spend less time searching, and more time being there for each other.
